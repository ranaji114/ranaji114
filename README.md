## 👨‍💻 About Me

I’m a curious builder who fell in love with code while trying to understand how things work. That curiosity led me to **Full Stack Development**, **AI research**, and eventually to designing my own programming language — **Ezra**.

My journey isn’t a straight line. I write code, but I also write books — from tech philosophy to poetry. I study computer applications, but I also publish research papers on the nature of time and quantum entanglement. I believe the most impactful work happens at the intersections, and that’s exactly where I want to live.

I’m motivated by problems that don’t have clean answers yet: How can we build systems that are both powerful and simple? Can a programming language make distributed computing as natural as writing a for-loop? What does it mean for a machine to “understand” context? These questions drive everything I build, write, and research.

When I’m not in front of a terminal, you’ll find me reading, sketching system diagrams, or refining the grammar of a language that one day might change how developers think.

---

## 🧠 Developer Philosophy

- **Simplicity scales, complexity breaks.** I design systems that are easy to reason about, even when the underlying problem is hard.
- **Code is a medium of thought.** Good code doesn't just run — it communicates intent to the next developer (which is often me, six months later).
- **Open source is not a feature, it’s a responsibility.** I build in public because shared knowledge accelerates everyone.
- **Research-driven development wins.** Tinkering is fun; hypothesis-driven tinkering is how you ship breakthroughs.
- **Tools shape thinking.** That’s why I’m building Ezra — to give developers a sharper tool for thinking about concurrency and state.
- **Ship the smallest useful version.** Perfect is the enemy of shipped. I believe in iterative learning through real-world feedback.
